Loop glyphs · v1

A glyph set anchored to the real mark, and a map that feels alive without lying.

Two things you asked for. Twenty-two custom glyphs on one 24×24 grid at one stroke weight, replacing the generic emoji that were carrying meaning; and a four-layer ambient treatment for the map, each layer implementable with a real service in production.

The mark is your file, not my trace. I pulled the path straight out of the SVG you sent, stripped the tile, re-anchored the relative start to M46 18, measured its bounding box by rasterising it, and normalised it into the 24×24 grid at a 19.4px live area — translate(-10.506 -4.767) scale(0.4743). Nothing is redrawn. Because the logo is a filled outline, the over/under crossings are already carved into its counters, so no casing is needed. Separately: the other twenty-one glyphs are line work, not illustrated emoji — painterly Apple-style art needs an illustrator.
The rule

Four glyphs are the logo itself. The other eighteen are line work on its grid.

24×24 grid, 20px live area. 2px trim on all sides so glyphs optically match SF Symbols beside them. 1.9px stroke, round caps and joins. Same weight as the existing TabIcons.tsx, so nothing looks imported. The mark is used verbatim. loop, vouch, mutual and invite carry the real logo path — filled, never restroked, never redrawn. State rides in a corner badge. The mark fills its box, so a tick or node sits bottom-right on a surface-coloured disc rather than inside the knot. Pass the surface colour in as a prop. Dashes mean weak. A dashed contour always reads “third degree / unverified”, matching the map's ring encoding. One accent per glyph. Teal for trust, gold for standing, rust for cost. Never two in one mark.

Living map · four layers

Ambient motion that never invents data.

The rule that keeps this honest: motion may respond to real coordinates and real degrees, and nothing else. No moving traffic, no fake activity pings, no drawn chain between people whose locations the app does not know. Everything here runs live — watch for about ten seconds.

6 homes · 4 within two steps © OpenStreetMap · CARTO
Layer 01 · light sweep · 11s Daylight crossing the city A soft-light gradient band travelling across the basemap. Reads as weather rather than UI, and carries no meaning at all — which is why it is allowed to loop forever. Prod: Skia LinearGradient + BlendMode.SoftLight in a <Canvas> over the existing MapCanvas, driven by one Reanimated clock.
Layer 02 · cloud drift · 34s One slow shadow A single large radial darkening, drifting on a 34-second cycle. Long enough that you never catch it moving, short enough that the map is never static. Depth without parallax tricks. Prod: same Skia canvas, RadialGradient on a withRepeat(withTiming(...)) translate. Or a MapLibre raster layer with animated raster-opacity if you migrate the basemap.
Layer 03 · reach wash · 7.5s Your reach, pulsing outward A teal ring expanding from your own pin. This one is data — its radius is the bounding circle of everything within two steps of you, so it grows as your network does. The only layer tied to the graph. Prod: deck.gl ScatterplotLayer with animated getRadius transitions, or Skia Circle with a shared progress value. Radius from the same domain function that computes degrees — never a constant.
Layer 04 · breathing pins + marching dashes Faces alive, links honest Pins breathe on a 5.6s cycle, staggered so the map never pulses in unison; ring weight still encodes degrees, and your own pin carries the one gold halo. Dashed links march slowly — and only ever between two listings, both of which have real coordinates. Prod: Reanimated on the existing marker views; links as deck.gl ArcLayer or a MapLibre line layer with animated line-dasharray. Cap at ~12 animated markers and drop to static past that.
Deliberately absent: moving vehicles, live “someone just joined” pings, heat maps of activity, and any line drawn between two people rather than two homes. Each would look great and imply data Kiki does not have.
