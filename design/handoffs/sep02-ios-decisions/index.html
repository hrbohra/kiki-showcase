Kiki · native iOS

Not a booking app. A way of proving you are worth letting in.

Every other stay platform starts with the room and bolts trust on afterwards as a star rating. Kiki inverts it: the person comes first, the room is a consequence, and the only currency is who will put their name next to yours.

Six decisions

The design job was not to make a marketplace look warm. It was to make an obligation legible.

Who vouched for you, what that costs them, and what you owe back. Sorting by degrees of separation instead of price is not a feature — it is the entire argument, and every screen below is built to hold it up.

01 Native-first Expo One codebase, real iOS gestures, safe-area insets honoured on every screen.
02 A pure domain layer Degrees, vouches and standing are computed and unit-tested, not faked per screen.
03 A bespoke map Web-Mercator over real listing coordinates rather than a dropped-in map SDK.
04 Honesty in the copy “Some will say no. That’s the system working.” Nothing softened to sell.

Note for Claude Code — scroll-driven screens. Wrap this showcase in a rail of N × 100vh with the stage position:sticky; top:0; height:100vh, and set the screen index from -rail.getBoundingClientRect().top / (rail.offsetHeight - innerHeight). Two things broke it here: no ancestor may create a scroll container (overflow-x:hidden silently computes overflow-y:auto — use clip), and the pinned child must fit the viewport exactly, so the phone has to be scaled against the height left over after the heading and picker.
